# Independent task set: Blender 4.5 Python scripting

Ten requests of the kind people give a coding agent. Each asks for one Python script that runs headless as

```
"$BLENDER" -b --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python script.py -- OUTPUT
```

and writes exactly one file to `OUTPUT`, the first argument after `--`. Every task is checked from that file alone with

```
"$BLENDER" -b --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 --python checks.py -- TASK OUTPUT
```

which prints one `CHECK {json}` line and exits 0 on pass, 1 on fail. All tasks run on a CPU in well under a minute and need no network, GPU or add-on that is off by default. In image checks, pixel (x, y) counts from the top-left corner and values run 0–1.

**1. Terrain from a height table (modelling from data).** A survey gives me a heightmap as CSV: five rows for y = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 m (first row is y = 0) and seven columns for x = 0 to 6 m (first column is x = 0), each value a height in metres. The rows are `0.0,0.1,0.2,0.2,2.2,2.3,2.3` / `0.0,0.1,0.2,0.3,2.3,2.4,2.4` / `0.1,0.2,0.3,0.3,2.3,2.4,2.5` / `0.1,0.2,0.3,0.4,2.4,2.5,2.5` / `0.2,0.3,0.4,0.4,2.4,2.5,2.6`. Write a script that builds one mesh object named `Terrain` with a vertex at (x, y, height) for every value, in world space, and one quad per grid cell. Shade it smooth but keep the cliff crisp: any edge where the two faces meet at more than 30° must render as a hard edge, and every edge under 30° must render smooth (what older Blender called Auto Smooth at 30°). Save the scene as a `.blend` file to the path given as the first argument after `--`. Check: the evaluated `Terrain` mesh has 35 vertices at the table's positions (±1 mm) and 24 quads, and its per-corner normals are split (more than 5° apart at both ends) across each of the 8 edges steeper than 30° and shared (within 0.5°) across every edge gentler than 30°.

**2. Weighted bevel and array (modifiers).** Make a box 2 m in X, 2 m in Y and 1 m in Z, centred on x = y = 0 with its base on z = 0, as an 8-vertex mesh with no object scale left on it. Give its four vertical edges a bevel weight of 1.0 and its other eight edges 0. Then add these modifiers, in this order, and leave them unapplied: a Bevel limited by bevel weight, width 0.1 m, 3 segments, other settings default; and an Array making 3 copies along +X with a 0.5 m gap between neighbouring boxes and no vertex merging. Save as a `.blend` file to the path given as the first argument after `--`. Check: an object has a live Bevel modifier before a live Array modifier on an 8-vertex base mesh, and its evaluated mesh has 96 vertices spanning x −1 to 6 m and y −1 to 1 m (±5 mm), every vertex at exactly z = 0 or z = 1 (no horizontal edge bevelled), and each of the 12 vertical corners replaced by 4 points on an arc of radius 0.1 m (±5 mm).

**3. Coated car paint (materials and shading).** Add a UV sphere of radius 1 m at the origin and give it a new material for a coated, slightly see-through car paint. Its Principled BSDF, plugged straight into the Material Output's Surface, should have: base colour (0.05, 0.2, 0.6), metallic 0.6, roughness 0.4, clearcoat 1.0 with clearcoat roughness 0.08, sheen 0.3, transmission 0.25, emission colour (1.0, 0.5, 0.1) at emission strength 0.5, and alpha 0.9. For EEVEE, make the material render with blended (not dithered) transparency and hide the back faces of the transparent surface. Save as a `.blend` file to the path given as the first argument after `--`. Check: the radius-1 sphere's first material has a Principled BSDF feeding the active Material Output's Surface directly, with those ten inputs at those values (±0.001, colours compared as RGB) and none of them linked; the material's EEVEE render method is Blended and its transparency overlap (show backface) is off.

**4. Light linking (lighting and rendering).** Build this scene: a 10 m × 10 m ground plane at z = 0 and two UV spheres of radius 1 m centred at (−1.5, 0, 1) and (1.5, 0, 1), all three using one white diffuse material (Principled, base colour 0.8, roughness 1); a black world; and one sun lamp of strength 3 shining straight down. The sun must light the ground and the left sphere but not the right sphere: the right sphere stays in the scene and visible, it just receives no light from the sun. Camera: orthographic, scale 8, at (0, 0, 10) looking straight down, +X to the right of the frame and +Y towards the top. Render with Cycles on the CPU (32 samples is enough) at 400 × 200 px, 100 %, and save the PNG to the path given as the first argument after `--`. Check: the PNG is 400 × 200; the mean brightness of 9 × 9 px patches is above 0.4 at the left sphere's centre (125, 100) and at four ground points ((200, 20), (30, 100), (370, 100), (200, 180)), and at the right sphere's centre (275, 100) it is below 35 % of the left sphere's.

**5. 360° panorama (cameras).** Put a camera at the origin and render a full 360° × 180° equirectangular panorama, 512 × 256 px, with Cycles on the CPU (16 samples is enough). Around it, six 1 m cubes centred 5 m from the origin, each with an emission-only material of strength 1, and a black world: +Y red (1, 0, 0), +X green (0, 1, 0), −Y blue (0, 0, 1), −X yellow (1, 1, 0), +Z white (1, 1, 1), −Z magenta (1, 0, 1). Orient the camera so the centre of the image looks along +Y with +Z up, and the image reads the way a person standing at the origin facing +Y sees it (turning right faces +X). Save the PNG to the path given as the first argument after `--`. Check: the PNG is 512 × 256, and 3 × 3 px patches, classified by which colour channels dominate, are red at the centre (256, 128), green at (384, 128), yellow at (128, 128), blue at both side edges (2, 128) and (509, 128), white at four points on row 2, magenta at four points on row 253, and black between the markers ((192, 128), (320, 128)) and at (256, 192) and (64, 64).

**6. Looping slide, spin and glow (animation).** Set the scene to 24 fps with frames 1–96. Add a 1 m cube named `Hopper` at the origin with a Principled material. Keyframe its X location to 0 m at frame 1, 4 m at frame 25 and 0 m at frame 49; its Z rotation to 0° at frame 1 and 360° at frame 49; and the material's emission strength to 0 at frame 1, 5 at frame 25 and 0 at frame 49. All three must move at constant speed between keys (no easing) and repeat forever with a 48-frame period, so the cube keeps sliding back and forth, spins without stopping or reversing, and pulses. Save as a `.blend` file to the path given as the first argument after `--`. Check: the file is at 24 fps with frame range 1–96, and evaluating frames 7, 13, 25, 37, 55, 73 and 91 gives `Hopper`'s world X (±0.02 m), Z rotation (±1°, modulo 360°) and its material's emission strength (±0.02) equal to the linear, 48-frame-periodic values (for example frame 7 and frame 55: 1 m, 45°, 1.25; frame 91: 1 m, 315°, 1.25).

**7. Baked rigid-body drop (physics).** At 24 fps with frames 1–120, set up a 20 m × 20 m ground plane at z = 0 as a passive rigid body and a 1 m cube whose centre starts at (0, 0, 10) as an active rigid body with mass 2 kg and a box collision shape. Rigid body world: 10 substeps per frame, 20 solver iterations, default gravity. Bake the simulation for frames 1–120 so the result is stored in the saved file, and anyone opening it can jump straight to any frame without playing the simulation through. Save as a `.blend` file to the path given as the first argument after `--`. Check: the file's rigid body world is enabled and its cache is baked over at least frames 1–120, with 10 substeps per frame, 20 solver iterations and 24 fps; there is exactly one active body, of mass 2 kg, and at least one passive body; and jumping straight to frames 1, 25 and 120 puts the cube's centre at z = 10 (±0.02), about 5.1 (±0.3, one second of free fall) and 0.5 (±0.05, resting on the ground).

**8. Sphere grid with an exposed Scale (geometry nodes).** Write a script that builds a Geometry Nodes modifier which produces a 5 m × 5 m grid of 6 × 6 points centred on the origin at z = 0, puts an icosphere of radius 0.2 m with 1 subdivision (12 vertices) on every point, multiplies the size of those instances by a float group input named `Scale` (default 1.0) that appears on the modifier panel, and realizes the instances so the result is ordinary mesh. Set `Scale` to 0.5 on the modifier itself, leaving the node group's default at 1.0. Save as a `.blend` file to the path given as the first argument after `--`. Check: an object's Geometry Nodes modifier has a float group input `Scale` with default 1.0 whose value on the modifier is 0.5, and that object's evaluated mesh has 432 vertices, 12 around each of the 36 grid points, each 0.1 m (±3 mm) from its point.

**9. STL for a 3D printer (import/export).** Model a torus in metres: major radius 0.04 m, minor radius 0.01 m, 48 segments around the ring and 12 around the tube, centred on the origin and lying flat (the ring in the XY plane). Export it as a binary STL in millimetres with Z up, so the printer sees a 100 × 100 × 20 mm part. Write the `.stl` file to the path given as the first argument after `--`. Check: the file is a binary STL with 1152 triangles, its bounding box is 100 × 100 mm in X and Y (±0.2) and 20 mm in Z (±0.1) and centred on the origin (±0.5 mm), every edge is shared by exactly two triangles, the enclosed volume is positive and between 0.90 and 1.01 times that of an ideal torus with the same radii, and at least 95 % of the stored facet normals agree with the triangle winding.

**10. Composite over a brand colour (compositing).** Scene: an orthographic camera at (0, −10, 0) looking along +Y with +Z up, ortho scale 4, rendering 400 × 300 px at 100 %; and a 1 m × 1 m square plane centred at (−1, 0, 0.5) facing the camera, with a pure red emission shader (colour (1, 0, 0), strength 1). Render with transparent film and use the compositor to put the render over a solid background that comes out as exactly #1A2B3C in the saved file, then mirror the whole image left to right. Render with Cycles and save an 8-bit PNG to the path given as the first argument after `--`. Check: the PNG is 400 × 300; 5 × 5 px background patches at seven points (the four corners, (100, 100), (200, 200), (300, 250)) are opaque and equal #1A2B3C within 2/255 per channel; and pure red pixels (R > 0.9, G and B < 0.1) cover 10000 ± 600 px centred on (299.5, 99.5) ± 3 px, where the square lands after the mirror.
